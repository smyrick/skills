# Before and after

These are editable examples. Adapt the move to the claim the presenter can actually support.

## Fix the order before rewriting the copy

A draft opens with **Overview**, **Benefits**, **Our approach**, and **Key takeaways**. Each slide promises faster review. Buried in the speaker notes is a story the presenter actually knows: three changes reached one reviewer, one passed tests but broke an older client, and the reviewer added a check.

Try **Three PRs, one reviewer** → **Which change would you open?** → **The old client failed** → **Keep the failing case**. Now the first slide sets up a bottleneck, the second lets the room make a choice, the third shows what the tests missed, and the last gives the audience something to try. The generic benefits slide can go. These titles illustrate an order; use the speaker’s actual evidence.

## A review queue

**Before** — “Accelerating development velocity creates downstream challenges in the review process.” Three bullets explain that agents work in parallel, more PRs arrive, and reviewers have limited capacity.

**After** — Title the slide **Three PRs, one reviewer**. Show three pull requests. On advance, show all three entering the same reviewer’s queue. Drop the bullets. The count is part of this fictional scenario.

**Speaker note** — “Pause on the queue. Which PR first?”

## When three bullets earn their place

A workshop audience needs three things to attach to a review request. Cut the three cards headed “Context”, “Transparency”, and “Confidence” and their descriptions. The slide can say:

**What to send for review**

- Link the original issue.
- Highlight the changed line.
- Attach the failing test.

The speaker can explain why the failing test was hard to find. The list stays useful to someone who photographs it.

## A change of speaker

Finish the example with the old client rejecting a new field. Put **Who reviews the change?** on the next slide, with ample space. Then open the reviewer's view of the same change. The divider gives the new speaker a place to begin without reintroducing the whole system.

**Speaker note** — “Hand to Michael.”

When the ticket leads to a pull request, leave a small labeled ticket visible as the PR opens. Each keeps its own identifier. A smooth morph from the ticket ID to the PR number would teach the wrong relationship.

## A training question

**Before** — A slide gives the learner a scenario, the question, the answer, and a paragraph explaining the answer all at once.

**After** — Show the scenario and ask the question. Let the room answer. On advance, retain the scenario and add the answer with one short reason. This also works as two consecutive slides.

**Speaker note** — “Take two guesses. Reveal.”

## A number with a boundary

Suppose 8 of 10 participants completed a task during a pilot. Replace “Transformative results at scale” and three benefit bullets with **8 of 10 finished the task** above a row of ten marks, eight filled. Label it **Illustrative pilot · 10 participants** in this example. Keep the denominator and the pilot scope visible. A company-wide success claim would require different evidence.
