# Simplify slides

Give each slide one point and enough evidence to see it. Leave the story behind the evidence to the speaker. Edit in the chosen format and within the requested scope. For a critique, show specific cuts and replacement copy.

## Start with the story

For a new deck, sketch the sequence in working titles before choosing layouts. For an existing deck, read the slides and notes in order. Decide where the audience starts and what it should understand or do by the end. Silently finish “After this slide, they know ___” for each slide.

Open with a moment the audience recognizes. Follow one example while it carries the argument. Merge slides that repeat a point. Split a slide when its ideas need different explanations. Give each change of chapter a quiet section slide. **Who reviews the change?** can carry a speaker handoff before the reviewer’s queue appears. Close on the answer, decision, or question the talk has earned.

## Edit what the room sees

- **Write short, speakable titles.** Name the thing that happened or the question at stake. “Three PRs, one reviewer” gives the room more to work with than “Scaling organizational review capacity.” Read the titles in sequence and listen for jumps.
- **Choose the form to fit the point.** Three checks can be three short bullets. Use a real crop to show a product detail, a chart for a comparison, or a short flow for a handoff. A single question works when the audience should look back at the speaker.
- **Let the slide's job set its visual density.** A chapter opener can carry one image and a few words. On an evidence slide, give the decision and the values needed to make it the most space. Group supporting detail, keep labels and units visible, and move optional evidence to a separate view. Use [visuals and dense slides](references/visuals.md) to choose between generated artwork, real captures, and editable slide elements.
- **Cut language that could belong to any talk.** Cut captions that announce what the room can already see, ceremonial openings, unsupported benefits, and closing lines that repeat the title. If three cards say the same thing in different words, keep the strongest detail. Use the actual noun, action, or result. Keep a qualifier when cutting it would change the claim. Shorten the copy before shrinking type.
- **Use the deck controls for ordinary advances.** Remove standalone links or buttons that only jump to the next slide when the deck already has a forward control. Keep meaningful demo actions, such as opening a pull request from its card, revealing a result or exploring an optional reference.
- **Reveal on the speaker’s cue.** Give each press one change worth noticing. Keep the shared object, label or chart axis in view as its role changes. Consecutive slides with the same frame also work. A still should remain intelligible.
- **Give the focus room.** If a central phrase already carries the point, drop the duplicate header. Bring ideas forward one at a time. Restore a title when it names the concrete example.
- **Write notes for a glance.** “Point to the old client. Pause.” A divider might only need “Hand to Michael.” One or two fragments usually suffice. When the deck supports builds, match the cue to the exact reveal and update it when the reveal order changes. Leave notes empty when there’s nothing to cue. Discard cut prose. If the presenter asks for a rehearsal script, give it its own space.

## Check the result

Scan the titles and thumbnails together. Can you retell the story? For feedback on a specific frame, identify the edition, slide and reveal before editing. In presentation view, check the changed slides and their neighbors. Check the densest settled frame at the intended viewing size. Can the audience read the decisive detail before you advance? For a build, check the order and timing with the actual presenter controls. If only an outline was available, say what still needs a visual check.

For an external or branded deck, preserve the supplied template, audience-appropriate footer, and cleared claims and assets. Label fictional examples that could read as customer facts. Keep internal source links out of audience files, including embedded notes. Use the [brand and template guidance](references/brand-and-tools.md) when adapting supplied layouts or preparing a co-branded deck.
